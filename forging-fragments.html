forging fragments


an arts council funded project

forging fragments is a practice-led research project tracing the continuity between printmaking and blacksmithing through the history of hand tools. it is supported by arts council england.

the project began with a simple observation: the tools i used to make prints — gouges, chisels, brayers — were themselves objects of great material history. forged by hand, worn by use, carrying the marks of their makers. i wanted to understand how to make them, not just use them.

over twelve months i am training in blacksmithing, forging replica historical tools, and documenting the process through prints, photographs, and writing. this page is the record of that work.

supported by arts council england · national lottery project grant

hereford training course

first week at the hereford course. the forge is a converted barn — low beams, the smell of coal smoke and hot metal. we started with basic drawing down, tapering a rod to a point. it sounds simple. it is not simple.

the thing nobody tells you about blacksmithing is how much you rely on sound. the ring of the steel tells you its temperature almost as much as the colour does.

3 april 2026

on the seax knife

been researching early medieval knives — the seax. simple drop point, single bevel, usually with a fuller running along the spine. there's a version in the V&A that i keep coming back to.

started forging a version. it is deeply humbling.

14 february 2026

why blacksmithing

the printmaking and the blacksmithing aren't as separate as they look. both are about pressure, resistance, mark-making. in a collograph you force ink into texture. in forging you force steel into shape. the hand remembers both.
